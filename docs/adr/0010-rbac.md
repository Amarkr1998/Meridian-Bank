# ADR-0010: Role-Based Access Control

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-04

## Problem

Meridian Bank has clearly distinct actor types — customers, operations staff, compliance officers,
risk analysts, auditors, and administrators — each of whom should only be able to perform the
actions and see the data appropriate to their function. A banking platform without a disciplined
authorization model is one of the most common sources of real-world breaches (horizontal privilege
escalation — one customer accessing another's account — chief among them).

## Decision

Implement RBAC with six roles: `CUSTOMER`, `OPERATIONS`, `COMPLIANCE_OFFICER`, `RISK_ANALYST`,
`AUDITOR`, `ADMIN`. Each role maps to permissions on specific resources/actions, enforced with
method-level security in each owning service. In addition to role checks, every endpoint that
returns or mutates customer-scoped data enforces **resource-level authorization** — the caller's
identity is compared against the resource's owning customer/account, not just their role. See
[Security Architecture](../security/security-architecture.md).

## Alternatives Considered

- **Coarse role checks only (no resource-level check)** — rejected: this is exactly the gap that lets
  a `CUSTOMER` change an account ID in a request and reach another customer's data; role membership
  alone cannot express "this specific customer's own resources."
- **Attribute-based access control (ABAC)** — more flexible in principle, but adds complexity beyond
  what this project's fixed, well-understood role set needs; RBAC + explicit resource-ownership
  checks covers every scenario in this project's brief without a policy engine.

## Trade-offs

Gains: clear, auditable permission model; straightforward to test (see Phase 17 authorization test
requirements). Costs: RBAC alone is insufficient and must always be paired with resource-level
checks — a discipline that has to be applied consistently by every service, not just configured
once centrally.

## Consequences

- Every service implementing a customer- or account-scoped endpoint must implement its own
  resource-ownership check, not rely solely on the gateway or a shared library to do it for them.
- Role assignment and permission checks are owned by `auth-service`; introduced in Phase 2 and
  enforced incrementally as each domain's protected resources are built.
